The idea

Most flashcard apps have already decided things for you: the order the cards come in, what the grading buttons say, what the whole thing looks like. Cardbox hands those decisions back.

You do not have to use any of it. The defaults work from the first launch, and everything below is there for the moment you find yourself wishing the app did something differently.

What you can change

The grading buttons

  • Rename them, add them, remove them
  • Decide what each one does to a card’s schedule
  • Give each one its own colour
  • One button is a valid setup, if that is how you think

The cards

  • A front, a back, an optional hint, and images on either side
  • Plain word cards, or fill-in-the-blank sentences
  • Read a card aloud with your phone’s own voice, per card
  • Hold and drag to reorder
  • Park a card with a switch — it stays in the deck and keeps its progress, but stops turning up while you study

Reminders

  • A fixed time, on the weekdays you pick
  • Or a random one: your own list of messages, one chosen at random, at a random moment inside an hour window you set

The way it looks

  • Colour palettes, including ones you build yourself
  • Light and dark, and two visual styles
  • Font, corner roundness, text size, text weight
  • High contrast and reduced motion

What you count

  • Daily goal, streak, cards due, cards studied, recall rate, cards mastered
  • The last thirty days as a chart, and how each deck on its own is going
  • Every one of those can be hidden. No leaderboards, no social feed.

What it does not do

The things worth knowing before you pay, not after.

  • No sync between devices. Moving a deck to another phone means a CSV export, a backup file, or a deck code you paste into a message.
  • Phones only, in portrait. It runs on a tablet, but it was not designed for one.
  • No shared deck library. There is no marketplace of decks to download. You write your own, import a CSV, or paste in a code someone sent you.
  • No account, so no recovery. Nothing is stored anywhere but your phone. Take backups.

Everything on this list is deliberate. Each one is a thing the app would need a server and an account for — and neither of those exist.

Questions

Does it need an internet connection?
No. It works in airplane mode from the first launch onwards, permanently.
Is there a subscription, or anything to unlock later?
No. One purchase, and that is all of it. No advertising and no in-app purchases.
Does it do spaced repetition?
Yes — both Leitner and SM-2, if you want them. They sit alongside nine simpler orders, so you can also just shuffle, or drill your worst cards first, and never think about scheduling at all.
Can I bring decks I already have?
Yes, as CSV. You can export the same way, and send a whole deck as a short text code in any message.
What does it collect about me?
Nothing. Not as a setting you have to hunt for — there is nothing in the app that collects anything, and no server for it to be sent to.
Who made it?
One person. I am Czech and I am learning German, and every option in Cardbox is there because I wanted it for my own five decks. If something is broken or missing, write to me and you will get a reply from the person who wrote the code.
